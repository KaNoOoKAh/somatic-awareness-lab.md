# Editing Policy

## 1. Purpose
This policy describes how raw talk-to-text, edited text, and observation notes should be handled.

## 2. Raw Talk-to-Text
Preserve the raw description as closely as possible, including transcription errors, repetitions, pauses, and incomplete phrasing when relevant.

## 3. Edited Version
Provide a cleaned version for readability and research use while keeping the original meaning as close as possible.

## 4. Note at Top of Page
Include a short note at the top explaining why the edited version exists and what it is trying to clarify or compensate for.

## 5. Labeling
Each document should have a clear label such as:
- Upload Draft
- Raw Description
- Edited Version
- Research Note

## 6. Preservation
Do not remove important meaning from the raw text. Keep unusual wording if it may matter to interpretation.

## 7. Clarity
Edit only enough to improve comprehension, structure, and readability.

## 8. Comparison
When possible, place raw and edited versions in the same file so they can be compared directly.

## 9. Separation of Versions
Visually separate raw and edited sections using headings or dividers.

## 10. Context
Preserve context about movement, emotion, pressure, attention, or breathing if it appears relevant.

## 11. Uncertainty
If a phrase is unclear, note that it may be uncertain rather than forcing a meaning.

## 12. Research Use
Documents may be used for comparison of raw speech, edited text, and observational interpretation.

## 13. Non-Destructive Editing
Do not overwrite the original voice of the speaker. Keep the original as a reference.

## 14. Override Review Question
Before any exception, bypass, or special-case edit is considered, the reviewer must answer:

> What questions should be in this?

This question must be used to determine whether the proposed change, override, or edit request is complete, understandable, and properly authorized.

## 15. Emotional Clarification
When emotions or feelings are described, preserve distinctions and note differences carefully.

### 15.X Emotional Differentiation Question
For each emotion or feeling you list, describe what felt different about it.

Include:
- what you actually felt
- how each emotion was experienced in the body or mind
- how it differed from the other emotions you listed

At the end, answer:
- Do you still consider these descriptions to be different emotions or feelings?
- If yes, what makes them different?
- If no, why do they feel related or the same?

## 16. Movement Observation
When movement is described, note whether it seems automatic, guided, controlled, forced, or compensatory.

## 17. Upload Labeling
Use consistent file names, dates, and labels so documents are easy to identify and compare later.
