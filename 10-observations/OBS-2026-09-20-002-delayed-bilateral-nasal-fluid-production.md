## Observation 21: Altered Taste Perception During Carrot and Italian Dressing Consumption

Time: 14:57 HST
Date: 2026-09-20
Location: Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii

### Context

This observation occurred during Session S-2026-09-20-003 while continuing respiratory, facial, and cranial awareness exercises.

Food consumed:

- Raw carrots
- Wish-Bone Italian Dressing
- Dressing was at room temperature

### Observation

### Taste Perception Difference

#### Description

While eating carrots with Italian dressing, the dressing flavor appeared significantly weaker than expected.

#### Findings

- The dressing was difficult to taste while eating it with carrots.
- Another individual reported that the dressing tasted normal.
- Flavor perception appeared different between individuals at the time of observation.

### Learning

Taste perception may vary between individuals and may also vary within the same individual depending on current sensory conditions.

---

### Observation: Chewing and Swallowing Coordination

#### Description

While chewing and swallowing simultaneously, the throat felt less coordinated than expected.

#### Findings

- Swallowing did not feel as smooth or automatic as usual.
- The sensation created awareness of the throat during eating.
- The experience was noticeable enough to be remembered during the observation period.

### Learning

Eating and swallowing may provide useful opportunities to observe throat awareness and coordination.

---

### Observation: Roof of Mouth Taste Localization

#### Description

While attempting to identify the flavor of the dressing, the strongest taste sensation appeared to originate from the roof of the mouth before taking a drink.

#### Findings

- Flavor appeared more accessible in the roof-of-mouth region.
- Other oral regions appeared less involved in flavor perception during this observation.
- After taking a sip of dressing, flavor perception became less distinct.

### Learning

Different regions of the mouth may contribute differently to subjective taste experiences.

---

### Observation: Warm Sensation Following Dressing Consumption

#### Description

After taking a sip of the dressing, a warm or almost hot sensation was noticed.

#### Findings

- The sensation occurred despite the dressing being at room temperature.
- The feeling appeared associated with the oil component of the dressing.
- The warmth was perceptual and not confirmed with temperature measurement.

### Learning

Certain food textures or ingredients may create temperature-like sensations even when the food itself is not physically hot.

---

### Interpretation

This observation documents:

- Reduced perception of dressing flavor.
- Increased awareness of swallowing mechanics.
- Localized taste perception near the roof of the mouth.
- A warmth-like sensation following dressing consumption.

This observation does not establish:

- Changes in taste physiology.
- Changes in throat function.
- Changes in sensory anatomy.

Additional observations are required to determine whether similar experiences occur during future meals.

## Observation 22: Delayed Bilateral Nasal Fluid Production During Exclusive Mouth Breathing

Time: 15:33 HST
Date: 2026-09-20
Location: Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii

### Context

This observation occurred during Session S-2026-09-20-003.

At the time of observation:

- Breathing continued exclusively through the mouth.
- No intentional nasal inhalation had been performed.
- No intentional nasal exhalation had been performed.

### Observation

Changes in fluid production were noticed between the left and right nostrils.

### Description

Approximately one hour earlier, the left nostril began producing mucus or a clear, stretchy fluid.

The left nostril is the nostril that is typically perceived as the dominant side during nasal breathing observations.

Breathing continued exclusively through the mouth throughout this period.

At approximately 15:33 HST, fluid was observed exiting the right nostril for the first time during the session.

### Findings

- Left nostril began producing clear fluid first.
- Fluid production on the left side remained noticeably greater.
- Right nostril began producing fluid later in the observation period.
- Right-side fluid production appeared substantially smaller than left-side production.
- Subjectively, the amount exiting the right nostril appeared approximately one-tenth of the amount observed on the left side.

### Learning

Fluid production appeared asymmetrical during this observation period.

The left side showed earlier and greater production than the right side.

### Interpretation

This observation documents:

- Continued exclusive mouth breathing.
- Fluid production from both nostrils.
- Differences in timing and apparent quantity between sides.

This observation does not establish:

- The cause of fluid production.
- Whether breathing method influenced the timing.
- Whether the left-right difference reflects airflow, awareness, or another physiological factor.

Additional observations are required to determine whether this pattern repeats.
