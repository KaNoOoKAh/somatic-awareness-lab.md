Observation 1: Perceived Asymmetry Between Nostrils

During normal breathing, one nostril appears to contribute more strongly to airflow perception than the other.
Findings
One nostril is consistently perceived as more active during inhalation.
The opposite nostril appears less engaged or less detectable during normal breathing.
Attention can alter awareness of airflow through each side.
Learning
Conscious attention may influence the subjective perception of airflow distribution between the nostrils.

---

Observation 2: Bathtub Water Exposure Trial

During a previous bathtub observation, water entered the right nostril without producing a significant sensation.
When body position changed and water lightly entered the left nostril, a sensation similar to accidental water inhalation was immediately perceived.
Findings
Right-side contact produced minimal detectable response.
Left-side contact produced a rapid and recognizable sensory reaction.
Learning
The two sides of the nasal cavity may not produce equivalent subjective sensations under identical conditions.

---

Observation 3: Throat-Based Inhalation Awareness

Intentional focus on the throat during inhalation produces a different sensation than concentrating on the nostrils.
Findings
Breathing initiated from throat awareness feels more balanced.
Airflow feels more evenly distributed between both nostrils.
Greater activation is perceived in the posterior throat region.
The anterior portion of the throat produces weaker conscious control.
Learning
Different attentional focus points within the respiratory pathway may alter the subjective experience of breathing.

---

Observation 4: Shifting the Point of Respiratory Attention

Continued practice appears to make it easier to sense regions closer to the lungs during inhalation.
Findings
Less focus is required on the upper throat.
Respiratory awareness seems capable of shifting deeper into the chest.
Breathing sensations appear more directly connected to lower respiratory regions.
Learning
Repeated somatic practice may improve access to internal respiratory sensations and awareness of deeper bodily structures.
