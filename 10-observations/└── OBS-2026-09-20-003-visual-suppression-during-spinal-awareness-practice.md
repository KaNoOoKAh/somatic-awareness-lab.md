# Visual Suppression During Spinal Awareness Practice

## Context

This observation occurred during a standing body-alignment and somatic-awareness exercise.

The session focused on:

- Body realignment
- Spinal awareness
- Whole-body relaxation
- Internal body mapping
- Directed concentration
- Postural control

---

## Starting Position

- Standing on concrete ground.
- Arms hanging loosely at the sides.
- Neck allowed to settle naturally into its resting position.
- Attention directed toward whole-body awareness rather than individual body parts.
- Posture was adjusted only when necessary.

---

## Breathing Method

Throughout the exercise:

- Inhalation was directed through the mouth.
- Exhalation was directed through the mouth.
- Breathing remained intentionally monitored throughout the session.

No intentional nasal breathing was performed during this observation.

---

## Procedure

The exercise consisted of:

1. Relaxing the body.
2. Contracting selected body regions.
3. Relaxing those regions.
4. Directing awareness throughout the spine.
5. Moving attention up and down the spine repeatedly.
6. Increasing mental and physical awareness of spinal structures.
7. Attempting to relax every area of the body simultaneously.
8. Using spinal awareness as the primary reference point for posture and alignment.

---

## Observation: Development of Spinal Awareness

### Description

Repeated attention was directed through the spine in an attempt to increase awareness of spinal position and structure.

### Findings

- Awareness of the spine became easier to maintain over time.
- Repeated practice appeared to strengthen perceived awareness of spinal regions.
- The spine gradually became the central reference point for posture.

### Learning

Sustained attention appeared associated with increased awareness of spinal positioning.

---

## Observation: Visual Suppression Experience

### Description

During the exercise, an increasingly detailed internal representation of the body began to form.

Attention was directed throughout different body regions and eventually toward the head and eyes.

The eyelids remained closed and the vision that can remain on when the eyelids are down was turned off.

At one point, a state was experienced that felt different from ordinary eye closure.

### Findings

- Internal awareness remained active.
- A detailed internal map of the body could be maintained.
- Visual awareness appeared substantially reduced compared to normal eye closure.
- The experience felt different from simply closing the eyes.

### Learning

Internal awareness and visual awareness appeared capable of changing independently during this exercise.

---

## Observation: Increased Internal Body Mapping

### Description

As awareness was repeatedly directed upward through the head region, the internal image or map of the body became increasingly detailed.

### Findings

- The internal map became progressively clearer.
- Areas that previously felt absent or unclear appeared easier to identify.
- The internal image became more complete over time.
- Awareness of body-region differences became easier to detect.

### Learning

Repeated attention appeared associated with increased detail within the perceived body map.

---

## Observation: Detection of Alignment Differences

### Description

As the internal map became more detailed, differences between body regions became increasingly noticeable.

### Findings

- The body appeared more asymmetrical within the internal map than during normal daily awareness.
- Areas that felt disconnected, restricted, or difficult to perceive became more noticeable.
- The body felt less aligned within the internal map than it typically appeared during everyday activity.

### Learning

Increasing awareness may reveal patterns or differences that are not normally noticed.

### Interpretation

This observation records a subjective awareness of alignment differences.

It does not establish physical misalignment or anatomical abnormalities.

---

## Observation: Internal Map-Guided Alignment

### Description

After developing a stronger internal map, body position was adjusted based on information perceived within the map.

### Findings

- Physical posture adjustments were made using spinal awareness as the primary reference.
- The internal image appeared to provide information about body position.
- Simply changing posture did not immediately update the internal image.
- The image appeared to update only after awareness was reintroduced to the adjusted area.
- Repeated cycles of adjustment and renewed awareness improved consistency between posture and the internal map.

### Learning

Physical adjustments and internal awareness did not appear synchronized automatically during this exercise.

The perceived map appeared to require renewed attention before reflecting posture changes.

---
### Additional Observation: Transition From Surrounding Tissue Movement to Spinal-Centered Movement

#### Description

At the beginning of the exercise, attempts to move or focus on specific spinal regions appeared to produce responses primarily in the surrounding muscles and tissues.

The targeted spinal region itself was difficult to perceive independently.

#### Findings

- Initial attempts to focus on a specific spinal region resulted in noticeable responses from nearby muscles and soft tissues.
- The surrounding areas reacted more readily than the perceived spinal structure itself.
- Repeated concentration was directed toward the spinal region.
- Attention was repeatedly moved through the spine and surrounding areas.

#### Additional Findings

- As the exercise continued, maintaining attention on the spine became easier.
- Awareness of individual spinal regions appeared to improve.
- Movement gradually felt less dependent on surrounding muscular activity.
- The perceived center of movement appeared to shift toward the spinal region itself.
- The spine increasingly felt like the primary reference point around which surrounding structures moved.

#### Learning

Repeated attention appeared associated with a greater ability to distinguish the perceived spinal region from the surrounding tissues.

The exercise progressed from awareness of surrounding muscular reactions toward awareness of a more central structural reference point.

#### Interpretation

This observation documents a subjective change in movement awareness.

The experience progressed from:

```text
Surrounding Muscle Awareness
              ↓
Spinal Awareness
              ↓
Spinal-Centered Movement Awareness
```

This observation does not establish that individual vertebrae were moving independently. It records a perceived shift in awareness regarding where movement appeared to originate.

#### Importance

This observation is considered significant because it introduced a distinction between:

- Awareness of muscles and tissues surrounding the spine.
- Awareness of the spine as a central reference point for movement.

The distinction became increasingly noticeable with continued practice and concentration.

## Observation: Mouth Fluid Production

### Description

Approximately halfway through the exercise, the mouth began producing a thick, clear fluid.

### Findings

- The mouth remained open.
- Thick, clear fluid accumulated naturally.
- The fluid was allowed to leave the mouth without interference.
- Attention remained focused on spinal awareness rather than oral sensations.

### Learning

Oral activity became noticeable without deliberate attention being directed toward it.

---

## Observation: Lower-Leg Awareness Difficulty

### Description

Awareness of the lower portions of the legs proved difficult to maintain throughout the exercise.

### Findings

- Regions below the knees were more difficult to perceive than many other body areas.
- Repeated attempts were made to increase awareness of the lower legs.
- A clear connection through the legs was difficult to maintain.

### Learning

Some body regions appeared substantially easier to access through awareness than others.

---

## Observation: Foot Pressure Accumulation

### Description

As the exercise progressed, a strong sensation appeared to accumulate in the soles of the feet.

### Findings

- Strong pressure and sensation were perceived in the bottoms of both feet.
- Ground contact became increasingly noticeable.
- Awareness could be directed easily through:
  - Head
  - Chest
  - Arms
  - Hands
  - Abdomen
  - Spine
- Establishing a similar degree of awareness through the legs remained difficult.

### Additional Findings

- Awareness appeared able to move more easily through the upper body than through the lower legs.
- The feet remained highly noticeable throughout the session.
- Lower-leg regions remained comparatively difficult to connect with.

### Learning

Awareness appeared unevenly distributed across body regions during this exercise.

---

## Observation: Whole-Body Alignment Through the Spine

### Description

Once a stronger mental and physical connection was established, posture adjustments were guided primarily through spinal awareness.

### Findings

- Maintaining awareness of the spine appeared to improve overall posture awareness.
- Large differences could be perceived when alignment was adjusted through the spine rather than individual body parts.
- Whole-body organization appeared easier to maintain when awareness remained focused on spinal structures.

### Learning

The spine functioned as a useful reference point for maintaining awareness of body position during this session.
