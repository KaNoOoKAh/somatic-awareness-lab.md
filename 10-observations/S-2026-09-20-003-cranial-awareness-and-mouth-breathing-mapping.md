## Observation: Rear Head Awareness During Mouth Breathing and Closed-Eye Concentration

Time: 2:30p.m.HST (Approximate)
Date: 2026-09-20
Location: Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii

### Context

This observation occurred during continued mouth breathing following the post-wake observations.

Breathing remained focused through the mouth while attention was directed toward areas near the rear of the head.

### Observation

### Description

- Attention was directed toward the rear portions of the head.
- Continued inhalation and exhalation through the mouth were maintained.
- Attempts were made to increase awareness and sensation in the target region.
- Closing the eyes appeared to make concentration easier.

### Findings

- Awareness of the rear head region became easier to maintain while the eyes were closed.
- A clearer subjective path toward the upper portions of the head was perceived during concentration.
- With eyes open, maintaining awareness of the target region appeared more difficult.
- Reduced visual input appeared associated with increased ability to focus on internal sensations.

### Learning

Closing the eyes may reduce competing sensory input and make internal areas easier to observe and maintain attention on during breathing exercises.

### Interpretation

This observation documents a change in subjective awareness and concentration.

The observation supports:

- Increased ease of concentration with eyes closed.
- Increased awareness of sensations associated with the rear portion of the head.
- Increased ability to maintain attention on internally perceived regions.

The observation does not establish:

- Increased circulation to the brain.
- Changes in blood flow.
- Changes in neurological activity.
- Physical movement of energy within the head.
