# Continuous Learning Log: Pen Mechanics & Directional Control

## 1. Core Concepts: Why Pens Feel Different
* **Ink Viscosity:** Traditional ballpoint ink is thick and oil-based, requiring more downward pressure, which can make changing directions feel sluggish. Rollerball and gel inks are water-based or water-soluble, flowing quickly and making the ball feel slick or "icy" when turning.
* **Ball-Seat Friction:** The tiny ball sits in a metal socket. If you write at a very high or very low angle, the metal casing might drag on the paper, altering your control when moving in specific directions (like left-to-right vs. up-to-down).
* **Paper Texture (Tooth):** Rough paper provides feedback that helps control a slick pen, while ultra-smooth paper can cause a gel or rollerball pen to skate out of control.

## 3. Directional Control Exercises (Drills to Track)
To test how a pen handles directional shifts, practice these three shapes and log the feedback:
* **The Infinity Sign (∞):** Tests fluid, continuous directional changes and tracks if the ball skips or resists smooth curves.
* **Tight Zig-Zags (/\//):** Tests sharp, abrupt directional changes to check for hard starts or dragging metal.
* **Concentric Circles:** Tests consistent pressure distribution across a 360-degree rotation.
