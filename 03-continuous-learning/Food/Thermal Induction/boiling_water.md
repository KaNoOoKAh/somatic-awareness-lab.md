## 6. Thermal Setting Experiment: Stored Heat vs. Fluid Absorption

### The Goal
To observe how a metal pot stores and transfers its thermal energy *after* the primary heat source is turned off, using hot water as a visual and auditory indicator.

### Experimental Setup & Variables
* **Independent Variable:** The wait time (cooldown duration) before adding water.
* **Dependent Variable:** The visual/auditory reaction of the water (sizzling, rapid boiling, mild steaming, or quiet mixing).
* **Controlled Variables:** Use the same pot, the same initial stovetop heat setting, and the same temperature of hot water each time.

### Scientific Framework: The Three "Thermal States" You Might See
Depending on how long you wait, you will likely catch the pot in one of these three distinct energetic phases:

1. **The Flash Zone (Superheated Phase):** 
   * *What happens:* If you add the hot water too soon, it will instantly erupt into aggressive boiling or loud hissing steam. 
   * *The Science:* The pot's surface temperature is still higher than the water's boiling point. The metal is rapidly dumping its stored thermal energy into the first layer of water it touches.
2. **The Equilibrium Zone (Simmer/Equalization Phase):** 
   * *What happens:* The water hits the pan with a mild sizzle, and gentle, steady bubbles form without exploding into crazy steam.
   * *The Science:* The pot and the hot water are close to the same temperature. They are harmonizing and exchanging energy smoothly until they match.
3. **The Dissipation Zone (Passive Cooling Phase):** 
   * *What happens:* The water pours in completely silently. No bubbles form, and you might only see a tiny bit of lazy ambient steam rise.
   * *The Science:* You waited long enough that the pot already lost most of its stored energy to the surrounding kitchen air. The pot is now cooler than (or equal to) the hot water you just poured in.

   ——-

   ### 6b. Pre-Experiment Hypothesis
* **Early Dump (Superheated):** Rapid, violent energy transfer causes large steam bubbles to project aggressively outwards and upwards through the water column to the top.
* **Delayed Dump (Cooled States):** Weakening energy transfer results in much smaller, gentler bubbles, eventually leading to completely silent mixing.
