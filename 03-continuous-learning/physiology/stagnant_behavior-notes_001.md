### Molecular-Level Behavior When Nutrients Are Not Actively Processed
(Active Research Addition — Somatophysical Integration)

When nutrients enter the body but are not actively processed, several predictable molecular behaviors occur. These patterns mirror the somatic pressure‑shift phenomena documented elsewhere in this lab, offering a biochemical parallel to stalled or redistributed somatic states.

1. Molecules remain in precursor form
Without enzymatic activation, nutrients stay in their original, unconverted state.

Glucose remains glucose

Amino acids remain free-floating

Fatty acids remain unoxidized

This reflects a non‑shifted molecular state, similar to a somatic pressure state that never transitions.

2. Accumulation in extracellular spaces
Unprocessed molecules often build up in plasma, interstitial fluid, or lymph.
This creates:

osmotic pressure changes

fluid redistribution

localized heaviness or stagnation

A biochemical analog to pressure buildup in somatic regions.

3. Transport proteins remain unengaged
If nutrients aren’t processed:

transporters do not bind

channels do not open

carriers remain idle

This leaves the molecular “traffic system” in a low‑flow, low‑pressure state.

4. Enzymes remain inactive
Enzymes act as molecular switches.
Without processing:

no catalytic reaction occurs

no energy is released

no metabolic shift happens

This mirrors a somatic switch that never flips.

5. Energy gradients fail to form
Processing nutrients generates ATP, NADH, proton gradients, and membrane potentials.
If processing stalls:

gradients stay flat

no energy wave forms

no molecular “push” occurs

A direct parallel to somatic pressure stalls.

6. Diversion into storage or waste pathways
Unused nutrients are often rerouted:

glucose → glycogen or fat

amino acids → urea

fatty acids → triglycerides

These are passive fallback pathways, not active processing.
