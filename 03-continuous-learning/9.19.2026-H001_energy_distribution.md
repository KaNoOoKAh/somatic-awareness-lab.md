Purpose
This entry documents subjective observations related to frequency-based audio exposure, body awareness, perceived energy distribution, and conscious control of somatic sensations. The purpose is to capture experiential data for future comparison, validation, and pattern analysis.

---

Background

On the evening prior to this observation, frequencies at and below 352 Hz were played through left and right earphones before sleep. Subsequent observations focused on changes in body awareness, perceived energy movement, and voluntary control of internal sensations.

---

Key Questions

* Audio Processing and Bilateral Input
* Questions for future investigation:
* When audio frequencies are delivered independently to the left and right ears, should both channels produce equivalent neurological or physiological responses?
* Is conscious attention capable of influencing perceived activation or deactivation of sensory pathways?
* Can intentional positive, negative, or neutral feedback alter how auditory stimulation is experienced?
  
Somatic Control

* Questions for continued exploration:
Can focused attention increase the perception of control over bodily sensations?
* Does repeated practice improve the ability to direct awareness toward specific anatomical regions?
* Are observed sensations associated with neural attention mechanisms, muscle activation patterns, autonomic responses, or other physiological processes?

---

Observations
Observation 1: Increased Ease of Internal Awareness

Repeated practice of directing attention and perceived force or energy throughout the body appears to increase the ease with which sensations can be located and experienced.
Noted Effects
Greater awareness of localized bodily sensations.
Increased perception of internal connectivity between body regions.
Reduced effort required to produce previously learned sensory responses.
Learning
Consistent repetition may strengthen conscious access to bodily awareness and internal sensory perception.

---

Observation 2: Localization and Redistribution of Sensation

Strong subjective sensations can be generated in specific areas, particularly the shoulders.
Example
Sensation accumulated in either the left or right shoulder.
Sensation appeared capable of being voluntarily redirected to other regions.
Perceived movement was experienced throughout multiple body areas.
Learning
Focused attention may influence how sensory awareness is mapped and experienced across the body.

---

Observation 3: Anatomical Areas Requiring Further Practice

Areas with weaker perceived connectivity include:
Brain and cranial interior awareness
Rib and thoracic regions
Hamstrings
Posterior forearms
Top portion of hands
Spine
Upper rear cranial region
Areas with stronger perceived awareness include:
Back of the head
Temporal regions
Eyes
Nose
Various cranial sensations
Learning
Certain anatomical regions appear easier to perceive consciously than others. Additional practice may be required to establish consistent awareness across the entire body.

---

Observation 4: Directional Characteristics of Sensation

While relaxed and applying intentional inward, outward, or neutral force, sensations appeared to follow established pathways.

Findings
* Some directions of perceived movement felt natural and immediate.

* Alternative directional patterns required substantially more effort.
* Sensations associated with the arms were often perceived as:
Vertical (up/down)
Horizontal (side-to-side)
Learning

The nervous system may possess preferred sensory or attentional pathways that become reinforced through repetition and habit.
