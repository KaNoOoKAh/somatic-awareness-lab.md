## 2. Dynamic Pressure & Fluid Dynamics Framework

When a character or symbol is drawn or printed, dynamic pressure governs how ink exits the tool and spreads across a substrate.

### Governing Parameters
* **Hydrodynamic / Contact Pressure ($P_d$):** Pressure generated by spatial movement and tool force against the substrate.
$$\Delta P = \frac{F_{\text{applied}}}{A_{\text{contact}}} + \rho v^2$$
* **Shear Rate ($\dot{\gamma}$):** Velocity gradient within the liquid film during stroke execution ($\dot{\gamma} = \frac{v}{h}$, where $v$ is velocity and $h$ is ink film thickness).
* **Capillary Pressure ($P_c$):** Driven by surface tension ($\gamma$) and contact angle ($\theta$) into substrate pores ($r$):
$$P_c = \frac{2\gamma \cos\theta}{r}$$

### Forms of Dynamic Pressure by Execution Mode

| Creation Method | Primary Pressure Mechanism | Dynamic Mechanics & Shear Behavior | Substrate Interaction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Manual Nib / Flex Pen** | Direct mechanical contact pressure + Capillary draw | Nib tines spread under vertical force ($F_z$), increasing contact area $A$ and decreasing fluid resistance; ink flows via capillary action and gravity. | Non-Newtonian shear-thinning allows smooth flow under high shear at stroke initiation. |
| **Brush / Flexible Tip** | Viscous drag + Elastic compression pressure | Fiber deflection increases surface area $A_{contact}$. Hydrodynamic pressure forces fluid out of internal capillary channels between fibers. | High shear rate at fast stroke speeds; stroke width scales dynamically with normal force $F_N$. |
| **Ballpoint / Rollerball** | Rotational shearing + Hydrodynamic lubrication film | Rolling sphere transfers ink via continuous shear. Dynamic pressure in the nip zone keeps the ball lubricated and prevents metal-to-paper contact. | Shear-thinning (thixotropic) ink thins instantly under high rolling shear rates, then recovers viscosity on substrate. |
| **Thermal / Piezo Inkjet** | Pulsed acoustic / Thermal bubble expansion pressure | Micro-cavity pressure spike ($\Delta P > 10^5 \text{ Pa}$) ejects pico-liter droplets at high velocities ($v \approx 5\text{–}10 \text{ m/s}$). | Kinetic impact pressure transforms droplet into thin film upon contact, driving lateral capillary spreading. |
