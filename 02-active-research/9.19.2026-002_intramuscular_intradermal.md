Overview
This research entry documents observations made following routine vaccinations administered in the left arm, including an intradermal tuberculosis (TB) skin test.
The purpose of this investigation is to determine whether sustained somatic contraction techniques and focused body awareness influence the perception of fluid dispersion, tissue awareness, or localized sensations following injections.
This document records subjective observations only and does not establish a causal relationship between somatic exercises and vaccine absorption, immune response, or physiological distribution.

---

Background
Following administration of routine vaccinations into the left arm and a TB skin test near the forearm, no unusual sensations were initially perceived.
After returning home, a series of somatic exercises were performed involving sustained inward contraction and concentration on specific anatomical regions.
The exercises focused on voluntarily reducing the perceived surface area of the arm by applying continuous inward muscular and skin-directed force while maintaining normal activities such as walking, breathing, and movement.

---

Somatic Technique
Primary Method
The exercise involved:
Applying continuous inward-directed force to the arm.
Attempting to contract muscles, connective tissues, and skin simultaneously.
Maintaining the contraction for extended periods.
Continuing normal breathing patterns.
Performing the exercise while walking and during normal daily activities.
Secondary Method
After concentrating on the shoulder and deltoid region, focus was gradually shifted toward:
Upper arm
Elbow
Forearm
Hand
For the tuberculosis test site, concentration was directed primarily toward superficial tissues nearest the skin surface.
