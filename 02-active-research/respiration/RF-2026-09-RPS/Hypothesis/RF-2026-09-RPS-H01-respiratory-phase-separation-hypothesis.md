# Respiratory Phase Separation Hypothesis

## Hypothesis Statement

Inhalation, exhalation, relaxation, and muscular contraction may be consciously experienced as separate processes through repeated attention and practice.

If these phases are normally perceived as a single automatic process, deliberate separation may increase awareness and perceived control over each individual phase.

---

## Research Question

Can conscious practice improve the ability to:

- Distinguish inhalation from exhalation?
- Distinguish respiratory actions from muscular contractions?
- Distinguish active effort from relaxation?
- Maintain awareness during the transition between phases?

---

## Background

Normal breathing typically occurs automatically and may be experienced as a single continuous process.

Preliminary observations suggest that consciously separating respiratory phases may increase subjective awareness of:

- Airway regions
- Facial regions
- Throat regions
- Chest regions
- Internal body mapping

---

## Predictions

If the hypothesis is correct:

### Prediction 1

Repeated practice will increase the ease of identifying individual respiratory phases.

### Prediction 2

Body regions involved in breathing will become easier to perceive.

### Prediction 3

Transition points between phases will become more distinguishable.

### Prediction 4

Awareness of relaxation may become as identifiable as awareness of contraction.
