Easter Island: The Illusion of the Statue Cult
On Easter Island (Rapa Nui), the final collapse of order came down to a purely visual obsession. 
The rulers and priests forced the population into a hyper-fixation on the Moai (the giant stone statues). For decades, the populace didn't rebel because the visual landscape kept them completely captivated. The rulers convinced the people that as long as the statues were built larger, grander, and placed perfectly along the coastline, the society would remain stable. 
But it was a hollow visual trick. While the people were looking up at the majestic, imposing faces of the stone statues, the actual foundation beneath their feet was rotting. The forests were disappearing, the soil was eroding, and the resources were drying up. 
The population eventually realized that the grandiose visual "posture" of their society was a lie. In a sudden, furious snap, they turned on the elite and went on a rampage specifically targeting those visuals—they systematically toppled and smashed every single statue across the island, shattering the very image they had spent generations building.

Egypt: The Tragedy of Pepi II (Overthrown for Cosmetic Reasons)
If you want an example of an Egyptian population turning on a leader where the reason felt completely ungrounded—almost entirely based on visual decay and superficial reasons—look at the downfall of Pharaoh Pepi II at the end of the 6th Dynasty. 
Pepi II came to the throne as a child and ruled for over 90 years, making his reign one of the longest in human history. For the vast majority of his life, he was viewed as a stable, peaceful, and traditionally "good" leader who maintained the cosmic balance (Ma'at).
However, as Pepi II aged into his late 90s, a bizarre psychological shift happened among the Egyptian populace:
The Visual Decline: In ancient Egypt, the Pharaoh was required to look like a youthful, vigorous, flawless god. It was a purely aesthetic standard of power. As Pepi II became frail, senile, and physically weak, the literal image of the divine ruler shattered.
Turning for No Good Reason: The population didn't turn on him because of a massive administrative crime he committed. They turned because he looked old, and they associated his physical aging with the land's environmental decay (the ongoing droughts).

The Superficial Snap: To the common people, the visual illusion of a perfect god-king was broken. Because they could no longer visually respect a frail ruler, the respect for protocol and administrative order dissolved entirely. His death immediately triggered the mass chaos of the First Intermediate Period, where the population flipped society upside down, attacked courtrooms, and desecrated tombs simply because the visual illusion of absolute authority had faded.
