1. Title
Somatic Pressure Displacement During Restricted Breathing Episode around the year 2023

2. Research Context
This entry documents a spontaneous somatic event involving restricted breathing, throat soreness, shallow inhalation, and light‑headedness. The purpose is to analyze the internal sensations, mechanical responses, and subjective resolution process using somatic‑awareness methodology.

3. Initial Conditions
Throat soreness

Shallow breathing

Difficulty achieving a full inhale

Light‑headedness

Sensation of internal tightness or “closed” airway pathways

No coughing, mucus, or external obstruction

Symptoms appeared suddenly and were not associated with exercise, illness, or environmental irritants.

4. Intervention Method
The following self‑directed technique was performed:

Deep inhale (as much as possible despite restriction)

Breath hold to increase internal pressure

Mouth opening to widen the pharyngeal space

Air gulping — voluntary swallowing of air

Repetition of the inhale → hold → gulp cycle

Somatic monitoring of internal sensations during each cycle

Additional Variation Performed
This pressure‑displacement technique was also performed using nasal inhalation instead of mouth inhalation.
The same sequence occurred:

nasal inhale

breath hold

mouth opening

air gulping

repeated cycles

The nasal‑inhale variation produced the same internal expansion sensation, suggesting the mechanism is related to pressure redistribution rather than the specific entry point of airflow.

Subjective sensation:
A progressive feeling of internal expansion, “opening,” and pressure redistribution within the thoracic and upper‑abdominal regions.
