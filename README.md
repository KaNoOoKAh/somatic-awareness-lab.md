# Somatic Awareness Lab

#### Created after HTTPS://www.github.com/kanoookah/somatic-cognitive-function

## Mission

Somatic Awareness Lab exists to investigate whether modern humans possess unrecognized physical, sensory, motor, and awareness deficiencies that become visible through directed observation, experimentation, and deliberate practice.

The central premise of this repository is that many functions of the body operate automatically and therefore escape conscious examination.

This project seeks to determine:

- What functions are operating efficiently.
- What functions are operating inefficiently.
- What functions may be underdeveloped.
- What functions may have become disconnected from conscious awareness.
- How the body absorbs and disperses mechanical, sensory, and postural energy — and why actively controlled directional sensory/concentration influences how that energy can be applied to internal processes.
- Why certain movements produce sensations that feel irregular, unstable, or inconsistent — and why applying a constant and stable form of direction-specific attention & control while applying directed sensory focus can normalize those patterns.
- To examine whether applying deliberate directional cues or controlled sensory activation to the spinal cord–cranium axis reduces motor irregularities and increases ease, fluidity, or efficiency.
- Whether awareness of those functions can be improved through practice.
- **Whether direction-specific sensory control during breathing, eating, and drinking affects physical or mental addictive tendencies.**

# Working Premise

Many daily activities become automatic over time.

Examples include:

- Walking
- Breathing
- Posture maintenance
- Swallowing
- Arm swing coordination
- Visual processing
- Nasal discrimination during respiration
- Acoustic localization and sound processing

Because these activities become automatic, individuals may lose awareness of how these processes are occurring.

This repository investigates whether conscious re-examination of these processes reveals overlooked inefficiencies, asymmetries, restrictions, or gaps in awareness.

# Current Hypothesis

The body may contain regions, functions, and patterns that have become underrepresented within conscious awareness.

If this is true, repeated observation and deliberate practice may:

1. Increase awareness of those regions
2. Reveal previously unnoticed limitations or deficiencies
3. Demonstrate that directional sensory control and focused attention can reorganize motor patterns, respiratory efficiency, postural stability, and pressure distribution
4. Show measurable improvements in physical performance, sensory discrimination, and subjective ease
5. Indicate that systematic practice on isolated body systems produces cascading effects across multiple physiological domains

# Important Distinction

This repository documents:

- Observations
- Experiments
- Hypotheses
- Procedures
- Analysis

Observations are not conclusions.

A sensation, perception, or experience does not automatically establish a physiological cause.

The purpose of this repository is to collect evidence, identify patterns, and generate testable questions rather than assume answers.

# Interconnected Relationships

The active research projects are not isolated studies. They interconnect through shared mechanisms:

### Directional Sensory Control
All projects (respiratory, acoustic, cranio-cervical, pressure dynamics) explore how directed attention and controlled sensory focus reorganize somatic function.

### Proprioceptive Integration
Respiratory awareness, cranial positioning, pressure mapping, and acoustic perception all contribute to refined internal body mapping.

### Pressure-Attention Coupling
Somatic pressure dynamics, respiratory function, and acoustic resonance all appear to be mediated by how pressure is perceived and directed through focused attention.

### Motor-Sensory Feedback Loops
Improvements in one domain (e.g., respiratory discrimination) produce changes in related domains (e.g., postural ease, cranial-cervical mobility).

### Cross-Domain Practice Effects
Practice on isolated systems (single nasal passage breathing, directional acoustic focus, localized pressure awareness) generalizes to broader functional improvements.

# Long-Term Goal

To create a structured body of observations that helps answer three questions:

1. What can the human body currently do?
2. What abilities, awareness, or functions are being overlooked?
3. Can those functions be improved through observation, practice, and deliberate training?

The objective is not simply to document the body as it exists today.

The objective is to investigate what aspects of human awareness, coordination, perception, and physical function remain unexplored—and whether systematic, directional-sensory-based practice can unlock previously inaccessible capabilities.
